# 1. Introduction

## 1.1 Welcome to QuantumCloud Technologies Inc.

Welcome to QuantumCloud Technologies Inc., an innovative technology organization dedicated to delivering modern cloud computing, software engineering, artificial intelligence, and enterprise technology solutions.

At QuantumCloud, we believe that our greatest strength lies in our employees. We are committed to building an environment where individuals can develop their professional skills, collaborate with talented colleagues, maintain a healthy work-life balance, and contribute meaningfully to the organization's success.

This handbook provides employees with a centralized reference for workplace expectations, organizational policies, employee benefits, and operational procedures.

## 1.2 Purpose of This Handbook

The primary objectives of this handbook are to:

- Communicate organizational expectations and workplace standards.
- Explain employee benefits and leave entitlements.
- Establish consistent guidelines for remote and in-office work.
- Promote a respectful, inclusive, and productive work environment.
- Clarify employee responsibilities regarding company resources and information.
- Provide guidance on accessing HR support.

All employees are encouraged to review this handbook carefully and consult Human Resources whenever clarification is required.

## 1.3 Scope and Applicability

This handbook is intended for employees of QuantumCloud Technologies Inc.

Certain benefits, including the annual learning stipend, apply specifically to full-time employees. Other provisions may depend on an employee's role, location, employment agreement, or applicable legal requirements.

Where local employment law or a signed employment agreement provides a different entitlement, the applicable legal or contractual requirement will govern.

# 3. Remote Work and Hybrid Workplace Policy

## 3.1 Policy Overview

QuantumCloud Technologies Inc. supports a hybrid working model that balances employee flexibility with in-person collaboration.

**All engineers are allowed to work remotely three days per week.**

The organization recognizes that engineering tasks frequently require focused individual work, while team discussions, planning sessions, and collaborative problem-solving can benefit from in-person interaction.

## 3.2 Designated In-Office Collaboration Days

**Tuesdays and Thursdays are designated in-office collaboration days.**

Engineers are expected to attend the office on these two days to participate in collaborative activities, including:

- Engineering team meetings and sprint planning.
- Architecture reviews and technical design discussions.
- Cross-functional coordination.
- Knowledge-sharing sessions and technical workshops.
- Project planning and retrospective meetings.
- Team-building and professional development activities.

## 3.3 Remote Work Schedule

Under the standard weekly arrangement:

| Day | Work Arrangement |
|---|---|
| Monday | Remote |
| Tuesday | In Office |
| Wednesday | Remote |
| Thursday | In Office |
| Friday | Remote |

This schedule reflects the standard hybrid arrangement for engineers.

## 3.4 Remote Work Responsibilities

Employees working remotely should maintain a professional and productive working environment.

Recommended expectations include:

1. Maintaining reliable internet connectivity.
2. Being available for scheduled meetings and agreed working hours.
3. Responding to work-related communications within reasonable timeframes.
4. Protecting company information from unauthorized access.
5. Keeping project management systems updated.
6. Attending required virtual meetings.
7. Communicating technical blockers and project risks promptly.

Remote work should not reduce the quality of communication, accountability, or project delivery.

## 3.5 Home Workspace and Equipment

Employees should establish a workspace suitable for professional activities.

The workspace should provide adequate lighting, reasonable privacy, and sufficient security for company devices and confidential information.

Employees are encouraged to follow ergonomic best practices, including using an appropriate chair, maintaining suitable screen positioning, and taking regular breaks.

Company-issued devices and equipment must be handled responsibly.

## 3.6 Exceptions and Special Arrangements

Employees who need an exception to the normal hybrid schedule because of illness, accessibility needs, emergencies, or other circumstances should contact their manager and Human Resources.

Any exception process should be documented and applied consistently, taking account of applicable law and individual circumstances.

# 5. Pet-Friendly Workplace Policy

## 5.1 Friendly Dogs Welcome

QuantumCloud Technologies Inc. maintains a strict **"Friendly Dogs Welcome"** policy.

The company recognizes that allowing suitable dogs in the workplace can contribute to a welcoming office atmosphere and improve the employee experience.

However, pet access must be balanced with employee safety, hygiene, accessibility, and comfort.

## 5.2 Registration Requirements

**All dogs must be registered with Human Resources before being brought into the office.**

Employees should contact HR to complete the required registration process.

Suggested registration information includes:

- Dog's name and breed.
- Dog's age.
- Owner's name and department.
- Emergency contact information.
- Vaccination documentation.
- Relevant behavioral or health considerations.

HR may establish additional administrative procedures to support safe office access.

## 5.3 Vaccination Requirements

**All dogs entering the QuantumCloud office must be fully vaccinated.**

Employees are responsible for ensuring that their dogs' vaccinations remain current.

HR may request appropriate documentation confirming vaccination status.

Dogs that do not meet vaccination requirements are not permitted in the office.

## 5.4 Animal Behavior and Supervision

The Friendly Dogs Welcome policy is intended for dogs that can behave safely around employees and other animals.

Recommended owner responsibilities include:

1. Supervising their dogs at all times.
2. Preventing aggressive or disruptive behavior.
3. Cleaning up after their dogs.
4. Maintaining appropriate hygiene.
5. Keeping dogs away from restricted areas.
6. Respecting employees who are uncomfortable around dogs.

Employees remain responsible for the behavior and welfare of their dogs while on company premises.

## 5.5 Animals Not Permitted

**Cats and reptiles are currently not permitted in the office.**

This restriction applies regardless of whether an animal is domesticated, vaccinated, or accustomed to human interaction.

Any legally required accommodation involving assistance animals should be handled separately by Human Resources in accordance with applicable law.

## 5.6 Employee Comfort and Safety

Employees who have allergies, phobias, or other concerns regarding dogs should contact Human Resources.

The organization should make reasonable efforts to address concerns while maintaining a safe and inclusive workplace.

# 6. Annual Leave and Paid Time Off

## 6.1 Annual PTO Entitlement

**Employees receive 25 days of paid time off (PTO) annually.**

PTO is intended to allow employees to rest, travel, manage personal responsibilities, and maintain a healthy balance between professional and personal commitments.

Employees are encouraged to use their available leave throughout the year.

## 6.2 PTO Planning and Requests

Employees should coordinate planned absences with their managers to reduce disruptions to business operations.

A recommended leave request process includes:

1. Reviewing the available PTO balance.
2. Identifying preferred leave dates.
3. Submitting a request through the designated HR system.
4. Allowing the manager to review scheduling requirements.
5. Receiving confirmation of the leave request.
6. Preparing a handover for ongoing responsibilities.

The specific approval workflow and notice periods should be confirmed by Human Resources.

## 6.3 PTO Carryover Policy

**Employees may roll over up to five unused PTO days into the following calendar year.**

For example:

| Unused PTO at Year-End | Maximum Carryover |
|---|---|
| 2 days | 2 days |
| 5 days | 5 days |
| 8 days | 5 days |
| 12 days | 5 days |

Unused days above the five-day carryover limit are subject to the company's applicable leave administration rules and local legal requirements.

## 6.4 Illustrative PTO Calculation

Consider an employee with an annual entitlement of 25 days.

If the employee uses 18 days during the year, the remaining balance is seven days.

Under the carryover policy, the employee may carry forward five days.

Assuming the employee receives a full 25-day entitlement in the following year, the employee would begin with 30 available days, subject to any applicable carryover usage deadlines.

## 6.5 Leave During Critical Projects

Employees should communicate planned leave early when working on projects with important delivery deadlines.

Managers should plan resource allocation appropriately and avoid unnecessary barriers to employees using their PTO entitlement.

## 6.6 Emergency Absences

Employees experiencing unexpected personal circumstances should notify their manager as soon as reasonably possible.

Human Resources should provide guidance regarding the appropriate leave category and documentation requirements.

# 7. Learning and Professional Development Benefits

## 7.1 Annual Learning Stipend

QuantumCloud Technologies Inc. supports continuous learning and career development.

**Every full-time employee receives an annual $1,500 learning stipend.**

This benefit is intended to help employees develop new skills, expand their professional knowledge, and remain current with developments in the technology industry.

## 7.2 Eligible Learning Expenses

The stipend may be used for:

- Books.
- Conferences.
- Courses.

These categories may support professional development in fields such as software engineering, cloud computing, artificial intelligence, cybersecurity, data engineering, leadership, and project management.

## 7.3 Illustrative Learning Budget

An employee might allocate the annual stipend as follows:

| Learning Activity | Example Expense |
|---|---:|
| Cloud architecture course | $450 |
| Technical conference | $600 |
| Professional books | $150 |
| Advanced programming course | $300 |
| **Total** | **$1,500** |

This example illustrates one possible allocation of the benefit and does not establish additional eligibility rules.

## 7.4 Learning Expense Administration

Human Resources and Finance should publish clear procedures for using the stipend.

These procedures should explain whether expenses require advance approval, how employees submit receipts, when reimbursements are processed, and whether unused stipend balances expire.

Until these details are formally established, employees should confirm the applicable process before incurring expenses.

## 7.5 Knowledge Sharing

Employees are encouraged to share useful knowledge gained through professional development.

Examples include presenting lessons from conferences, conducting internal workshops, writing technical documentation, or mentoring colleagues.

Knowledge sharing strengthens organizational capabilities and increases the long-term value of learning investments.

# 8. Information Security and Confidentiality

## 8.1 Employee Responsibilities

All employees are responsible for protecting company information, customer information, intellectual property, and internal systems.

Confidential information must be accessed and used only for legitimate business purposes.

## 8.2 Password and Authentication Security

Recommended security practices include:

- Using strong and unique passwords.
- Enabling multi-factor authentication where supported.
- Avoiding password sharing.
- Locking devices when unattended.
- Reporting suspicious login activity.
- Using company-approved password management solutions.

## 8.3 Remote Work Security

Remote employees should exercise particular care when accessing company systems outside the office.

Employees should avoid exposing sensitive information on shared screens or in public locations.

Company-approved security tools and secure network connections should be used whenever required by IT.

## 8.4 Data Classification and Handling

Company information may include public, internal, confidential, and restricted material.

Employees should follow applicable classification requirements when storing, transmitting, or sharing information.

Sensitive company data should not be uploaded to unapproved external platforms or personal accounts.

## 8.5 Security Incident Reporting

Employees should report suspected security incidents promptly through the designated IT or security reporting channel.

Examples include lost company devices, phishing attempts, unauthorized access, accidental data exposure, or suspicious system activity.
